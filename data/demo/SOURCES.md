# Offline Earth Engine snapshots

Central Delhi (28.62° N, 77.23° E) and central Bengaluru (12.97° N, 77.59° E) each cover a 5 km radius on a 500 m clipped grid. Cell files contain the live June, July and August extraction for 2019–2021. Monthly files contain all 36 months in the same years. The application computes scores and fits models locally from these observations.

Cell measurements use USGS Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 surface temperature, Copernicus Sentinel-5P OFFL NO₂, harmonized Sentinel-2 surface reflectance with Google Cloud Score+, Dynamic World, 2020 WorldPop counts and NASA SRTM terrain. Monthly temperatures come from Terra MOD11A2. [Earth Engine Data Catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets)

Dynamic World is produced by Google in partnership with National Geographic Society and the World Resources Institute and contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data. [Dynamic World attribution](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_DYNAMICWORLD_V1) · [WorldPop](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/WorldPop_GP_100m_pop)

The summer sensor check in `summer_landsat_monthly.csv` covers each June, July and August from 2019 through 2021. For each 5 km circle, Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 L2SP uses the map’s ST_B10 scaling and QA_PIXEL mask. Unlike the map’s all-summer pixel median, the check first takes the mean of each pixel’s valid temperatures within each calendar month, then averages over the observed area on the projected 30 m grid. MOD11A2 uses daytime 8-day composites assigned by start date, LST_Day_1km × 0.02 − 273.15, positive raw values and QC_Day bits 0–1 equal to zero. Its monthly mean is reduced across the same circle using the app’s 100 m sampling of native 1 km pixels. Blank temperatures mean no valid reading. Matched gaps weight each overlapping month equally. Shared area and shared gap use both sensors’ monthly valid masks on the 30 m grid; sampling a MODIS pixel more finely adds no spatial detail and does not match acquisition dates.
